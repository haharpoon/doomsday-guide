## Rule of Three and SOS

![SOS: three short, three long, three short](img/signals-sos.png)

Three of anything, repeated, means "I need help". Three whistle blasts, three flashes, three fires, three piles of stones. One shout is a person talking. Three is a person asking. The US Army survival manual puts it that way because nature rarely lines things up in threes.

SOS in Morse is ... --- ... Send it as one block: three shorts, three longs, three shorts, with no gap between the letters.

1. Send the signal.

2. Stop for about a minute. Listen and look.

3. Repeat until someone reaches you, or until you must save your breath or the battery.

Spend the effort when you can see or hear an aircraft, a boat, or people. Between those moments, rest, listen, and keep the signal ready.

On a hill, also use six blasts in a minute. That is on the Sound topic. At sea, a continuous blast on a horn is distress as well.

Do not practise a distress signal where a stranger might report it. A false alarm can pull a rescue team off a real one.

## UK Emergency Numbers

Call 999 or 112. Both are the UK's national emergency numbers. 112 is also the pan-European number, and it works here. The call is answered with "emergency, which service?" Ask for police, fire, ambulance, or coastguard.

On a hill, Mountain Rescue England and Wales and Mountaineering Scotland both say: ask for the police, then mountain rescue. Have ready:

- Where you are. A six-figure grid reference and a named feature if you can.
- How many people, and who is hurt.
- What is wrong, and whether they are getting worse.
- Your phone number, and any other number in the group.

Stay where you are once you have asked for help, unless the spot itself is dangerous. If you must call again, give the same details in full.

If you cannot speak, whisper, cough, or tap. From a mobile, if you stay silent, press 55 when the recording asks. Police.uk says that transfers the call to the police. 55 works on mobiles. It does not give your location. If you do not press 55, the call is ended. From a landline, silence with background noise is passed to the police, and the line can stay open for a short time if you hang up. A landline call carries location.

emergencySMS is for people who are deaf or cannot speak. Register before you need it. Text "register" to 999 and follow the reply. An unregistered phone cannot use it. In the message, name the service and the place.

Non-emergency: 101 for the police, 111 for the NHS.

Write the numbers that matter to your household on paper. A dead phone cannot show you a contact list. The Comms plan topic has a wallet card for that.
