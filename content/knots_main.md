## Rope words

Standing part: the long part of the rope that takes the load.
Working end: the end you tie with. The tail is what is left over.
Bight: a U-fold in the rope. The two sides do not cross.
Loop: a ring where the rope crosses itself.
Turn: the rope goes once round a post. Round turn: it goes all the way round, so two strands touch the post.

Hitch: ties a rope to a thing. Bend: joins two ropes. Loop knot: a fixed eye. Stopper: a lump in the end.

Dress it: lay every strand neat, no twists or crossed turns.
Set it: pull every part tight, then load it gently before you trust it.
Tail: a hand long. Short tails pull out.

## Square knot

Use: tie the two ends of one cord round something: a bundle, a sack, a bandage.
Do not use: to join two ropes that will take a load. It can slip or spill. Use a sheet bend.

1. Right end over the left end, and under. Pull snug.
2. Left end over the right end, and under.
3. Pull both ends and both standing parts.

Check: each end lies beside its own standing part, inside the knot. It looks flat. If the ends stick out sideways it is a granny knot: redo step 2 the other way.

Remember: right over left, then left over right.

## Sheet bend

Use: join two ropes, even of different thickness. Tie a cord to a tarp corner or a loop.
Do not use: on slippery or very different ropes, or one that will flap with no load. Use the double sheet bend (Bends and joins).

1. Make a bight in the thicker rope.
2. Pass the thin end up through the bight, from underneath.
3. Take it round behind both legs of the bight.
4. Tuck it under itself, across the bight. Not back through the bight.
5. Pull both standing parts.

Check: both short ends leave on the same side. Ends on opposite sides is weaker: retie.

![sheet bend: thin end up through a bight, round behind, under itself](img/knot_sheet_bend.png)

## Bowline

Use: a fixed loop in the end of a rope: round a tree or post, through a ring, a haul loop.
Do not use: to hold a person on its own. With no load it can shake loose. Use a figure-eight follow-through.

1. Make a small loop in the standing part. The side going to the end lies on top.
2. Leave enough end for the big loop. Pass the end up through the small loop, from behind.
3. Take the end round behind the standing part.
4. Back down through the small loop, beside itself.
5. Hold the end against the loop. Pull the standing part.

Check: the tail ends up inside the big loop. Pulling the standing part does not shrink the loop.

## Figure-eight loop

Use: a strong fixed loop you can check by eye: a ridge line end, a haul loop, a clip-in point.
Do not use: where you need to untie it easily after a heavy load. It jams. Need it round a tree or through a harness? Use the figure-eight follow-through (Loops).

1. Fold a bight a forearm long. Treat the doubled rope as one.
2. Make a loop with it, the bight crossing on top.
3. Take the bight round behind both standing parts.
4. Push the bight down through the first loop.
5. Dress it: the two strands lie side by side all the way, never crossing.

Check: you can see a clear doubled 8. Pull all four strands. Tail a hand long.

![figure-eight loop tied in a bight](img/knot_fig8_loop.png)

## Clove hitch

Use: a quick hitch on a post or pole. Start and finish lashings. A line you will adjust.
Do not use: alone for a heavy load, or one that jerks or turns the post. It slips and rolls. Add two half hitches.

1. Take the end over the post and round behind it.
2. Cross over its own line, on a slant, and round behind again.
3. Tuck the end under that slanting cross, so it leaves beside the second turn.
4. Pull both parts.

Check: on the front, one slanting strand crosses over two turns. The two ends leave from under it, in opposite directions.

![clove hitch: round, cross over, round again, tuck under the cross](img/knot_clove.png)

## Round turn and two half hitches

Use: tie a rope that takes a real load to a post, tree, ring or rail. You can tie it with the load on: the turns hold while you tie.

1. Take the end round the bar twice. That is the round turn. It does the holding.
2. Bring the end down beside the standing part.
3. Behind the standing part, over it, and under itself. That is one half hitch.
4. A second half hitch below the first, the same way round.
5. Slide both hitches up snug to the turns.

Check: the two hitches together look like a clove hitch on the standing part. If they mirror each other, retie the second one the other way.

![round turn and two half hitches](img/knot_round_turn.png)

## Taut-line hitch

Use: a guy line you can tighten and loosen: tent and tarp guys, a sagging washing line.

1. Pass the end round the stake, or through the tarp loop, and back up beside the line.
2. Wrap the end twice round the line, inside the loop, working toward the stake.
3. Bring the end up over those wraps, to outside the loop.
4. Once round the line, and tuck it under itself.
5. Dress the wraps side by side. Slide the knot up the line to tighten.

Check: pull the guy line hard: the knot holds. Push the knot: it slides. On slippery or cold cord add a third wrap inside the loop.
