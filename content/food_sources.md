Food Standards Agency cooking: food.gov.uk/safety-hygiene/cooking-your-food

Wild Food UK (wildfooduk.com) plant and mushroom guides, facts only: chicken of the woods (avoid on yew), puffballs (cut every one; amanita eggs and earthballs), chanterelle and its lookalikes.

Woodland Trust: sweet chestnut and horse chestnut (Can you eat conkers?), beech, most poisonous UK mushrooms (death cap, destroying angel, deadly webcap), yew, deadly nightshade, lords-and-ladies, laburnum, ragwort pages.

Food Standards Agency natural toxins factsheet and GOV.UK biotoxin and phytoplankton monitoring: shellfish toxins are not destroyed by cooking; closures and warnings.

CDC Yellow Book: paralytic shellfish poisoning symptoms and timing.

NHS: poisoning, call 111 or 999; British Dietetic Association and food labels for rough calorie values.

US Army FM 21-76 chapter 8: edible insects and worms (cook all; avoid brightly coloured, hairy or smelly insects, flies, ticks and mosquitoes).

Eatweeds and Spearfishing UK seaweed notes: no common British shore seaweed is poisonous apart from deep-water acid weed (Desmarestia); cut, leave the holdfast.

Acorn leaching: standard practice described by the Woodland Trust and university extension services; tannins must be removed before eating.

Plantlife and RHS for ragwort, hawthorn, elder, blackthorn and rose identification.
