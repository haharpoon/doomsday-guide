## Shelter sources

Written in our own words from:

US Army ATP 3-50.21 Survival, chapter 6: shelter site, poncho lean-to and tent, field-expedient lean-to, debris hut, snow cave, tree-pit shelter, insulation and ventilation (public domain, armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/pdf/web/ARN12086_ATP%203-50x21%20FINAL%20WEB%202.pdf).

US Army FM 21-76 Survival (1992, public domain): shelters and cold weather.

NHS hypothermia: nhs.uk/conditions/hypothermia

Snow trench and quinzhee steps are standard winter-camping practice consistent with ATP 3-50.21 snow-cave guidance (wall thickness, raised bench, ventilation, carbon monoxide).

US Army FM 21-76 (1992) chapter 5: poncho lean-to and tent, swamp bed, tree-pit snow shelter, desert shade shelters (a 30-45 cm air gap between two layers; the double cover can lower the temperature underneath by 11-22 degrees C).

CDC winter storm safety and US National Weather Service: stay with a stuck car, run the engine about 10 minutes an hour with a window cracked, keep the exhaust clear (cdc.gov/winter-weather, weather.gov/safety/winter-during).

NHS and UKHSA Keep warm, keep well: heat rooms you use to at least 18 degrees C (gov.uk/government/publications/keeping-warm-and-well-staying-safe-in-cold-weather).

RoSPA and the Children's Burns Trust: hot water bottles with hot, not boiling, water, two-thirds full, replaced every two years.

Scottish Outdoor Access Code (outdooraccess-scotland.scot) for wild camping in Scotland; elsewhere ask the landowner.

Bat Conservation Trust: bats and their roosts are protected in Britain (bats.org.uk).

Wattle and daub, cob and thatch: written from Historic England and SPAB (Society for the Protection of Ancient Buildings) technical notes on earth walls and thatch, in our own words. Mix ratios vary with the soil: always test.

Igloo, hammock and root-ball notes: standard winter and woodland camping practice; check against ATP 3-50.21.
