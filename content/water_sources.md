## Water sources

Written in our own words from:

CDC: cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html (boiling, 3 minutes above 6,500 ft, bleach doses). US EPA: epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water

WHO: Guidelines for drinking-water quality; household water treatment.

SODIS / Eawag: sodis.ch/methode/anwendung/index_EN.html

US Army FM 21-76 / ATP 3-50.21 Survival (public domain, armypubs.army.mil): finding water, solar still.

Hesperian, Where There Is No Doctor (free non-commercial).

UK government Prepare: water supply interruptions: WHO minimum 2.5-3 litres to drink a day, 10 litres with basic cooking and hygiene; store bought bottled water (prepare.campaign.gov.uk).

US EPA emergency disinfection: 2% iodine 5 drops a litre, 10 if cloudy, 30 minutes; CDC: avoid iodine if pregnant, thyroid problems, or for more than a few weeks; iodine and chlorine do not kill Cryptosporidium.

WHO Guidelines for drinking-water quality, table 6.1 (travellers' methods): filters need 1 micron or smaller pores; most do not remove viruses; carbon does not remove pathogens.

CDC: cleaning and sanitising water storage containers with bleach (cdc.gov/water-emergency).

CAWST Biosand Filter Manual (Version 10): 5 cm standing water, at least 50 cm fine sand over separating and drainage gravel, biolayer takes up to 30 days, pause 1 to 48 hours (washresources.cawst.org).

US Army FM 21-76 chapter 6: water from vines and bamboo, beach wells behind the first dune, vegetation bag yields 0.5 to 1 litre a day.

First-flush sizing: Texas A&M rainwater harvesting and Grafman, To Catch the Rain: about 0.4 to 0.5 litres per square metre of roof, more for dirty roofs.

Blue-green algae toxins survive boiling: UKHSA / Environment Agency and NHS advice on blue-green algae.

Hot water cylinders and toilet cisterns as emergency water: US FEMA / Ready.gov emergency water notes, adapted for UK homes.
