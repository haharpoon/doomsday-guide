## Fire sources

Written in our own words from:

US Army ATP 3-50.21 Survival, chapter 5: fire triangle, site, tinder, kindling and fuel, fire lays, metal match, flint and steel, char cloth, bow and drill (public domain, armypubs.army.mil).

US Forest Service / Smokey Bear campfire safety: smokeybear.com/en/prevention-how-tos/campfire-safety

The Countryside Code: gov.uk/government/publications/the-countryside-code/the-countryside-code-advice-for-countryside-visitors

Dakota fire hole: described in US Army FM 3-50.3 Survival, Evasion and Recovery (public domain). Dimensions here are typical, not exact.

NHS burns and scalds: nhs.uk/conditions/burns-and-scalds

US Army FM 21-76 Survival (1992, public domain), chapter 7: hand drill, fire plough, bow and drill, lens, battery, chemical fire starting, fire site and reflector.

Ordnance Survey and Woodland Trust tree guides: birch, ash and woodland identification (woodlandtrust.org.uk).

Woodsure Ready to Burn and Defra seasoning guide: dry wood is 20% moisture or less; oak and ash heat best, willow and poplar are poor (readytoburn.org, uk-air.defra.gov.uk).

Potassium permanganate and glycerine: Royal Society of Chemistry demonstration notes on the delayed exothermic reaction (edu.rsc.org).

Lithium battery fire risk: London Fire Brigade and Office for Product Safety and Standards advice on e-bike and lithium-ion battery fires.

US Forest Service / Smokey Bear: drown, stir, feel; do not bury coals.

Fungus tinders (cramp ball Daldinia concentrica, horse's hoof Fomes fomentarius): identification from Woodland Trust fungi pages and standard UK bushcraft practice.
